Good morning. This is One Finance UX.

At close of business, a controller has one question per outcome. Can I execute this rec? Can I produce the fifteen C three report? Can I close the books?

Today we will create an outcome, drive it for this business date, and show both the good path and the blocked path.

We start on Onboarding. We name the business question, declare the input feeds, the SLA, and what to do when every feed is ready. This is data, not a new product module. When we onboard it, the outcome goes live immediately.

Configuration is where the outcome owner inspects the contract. Pick the outcome on the left. The right pane shows entitlement, SLA, the on-ready action, and every feed that must complete. Onboarding creates. Configuration governs.

For this business date, we start Drive. Drive is the testing console. We run the day's scenario for the outcome. The product pages stay clean. They only show the fold.

Here is the good path. Every feed arrives. The outcome moves from feeds, to ready, to processing, to generated, and available to view. A controller opens the report, and can proceed.

Here is the bad path. A required feed fails. The instance is blocked on a named key. The user sees exactly which source failed. They can escalate to run the bank.

For a C I O or managing director, the Outcome board is the management screen. Traffic lights across the unit. Ready, blocked, escalations. No book grid. No engine internals.

These screens are mobile friendly. The same traffic lights work on a phone between meetings. A mobile app can wrap this shell. The job does not change.

Run the bank monitors delays, escalations, and dead letters. They can replay a failed event. They do not sign off a rec. That stays with the outcome user.

One platform. Two models. Outcomes fold readiness. Console kits host the human work. New capabilities launch by configuration, not new code.
